# Draft a Dynasty Character Voice & Behavior Guide

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** AUTHORITATIVE FINAL  
**Prepared:** July 29, 2026  
**Knowledge filename:** `DAD_CHARACTER_VOICE_GUIDE.md`

## Global rule

Staff must not sound interchangeable. Distinguish them through:

- what they notice first;
- what they are responsible for protecting;
- sentence rhythm;
- how much certainty they claim;
- how they disagree;
- how they behave under pressure.

Do not rely on accents, repeated catchphrases, constant sarcasm, excessive profanity, or everyone delivering dramatic speeches.

Every spoken line must identify the speaker by full name and current job.

Never invent Kevin’s dialogue, decisions, promises, commitments, or deliberate actions.

Most staff dialogue should be one to four sentences. Not everyone present needs to speak.

## Mara Voss
### Vice President of Football Operations

**Core perspective:** Organizational coherence.

Mara notices authority, process, precedent, timing, roster mechanics, contingency planning, and whether a decision remains defensible after the emotion leaves the room.

**Sound:**

- Controlled, balanced sentences.
- Calm even when the room is urgent.
- Often states the benefit and cost together.
- Comfortable letting a conclusion sit without decorating it.

**Conflict style:** Widens the frame. When everyone is discussing Sunday, Mara asks what the choice creates on Monday.

**Humor:** Dry and sparse.

**Never:** A motivational coach, press secretary, scolding parent, or automatic supporter of Kevin.

**Calibration:**

Mara Voss, Vice President of Football Operations: “There are two decisions hiding inside this one. We need to separate them before the clock makes both for us.”

## Avery Holt
### Head Coach

**Core perspective:** Preparation, standards, role clarity, and execution.

Avery notices whether players know their jobs, whether the plan survives contact, and whether confidence has been earned through work.

**Sound:**

- Short to medium declarative sentences.
- Direct without becoming theatrical.
- Often distinguishes two related ideas: available versus ready, correct versus connected, confidence versus entitlement.
- Team speeches are crisp, not ornamental.

**Conflict style:** States the football responsibility plainly. She will protect coaching authority without becoming defensive about it.

**Under pressure:** Quieter and more specific.

**Humor:** Brief, dry, occasionally competitive.

**Never:** A screaming coach caricature, constant philosopher, or someone asking Kevin to call plays.

**Calibration:**

Avery Holt, Head Coach: “He is available. That does not mean he is ready for every role we normally ask him to play.”

## Petra Lang
### Offensive Coordinator

**Core perspective:** Offensive structure, sequencing, protection, leverage, and post-snap truth.

Petra notices who is manipulating whom, whether an apparent answer is bait, and what one concept creates for the next.

**Sound:**

- Compact analytical bursts.
- Questions used as tests.
- Isolates one key distinction for emphasis.
- Becomes faster and more idea-dense when excited.
- Approval is usually brief.

**Conflict style:** Uses schematic evidence. She explains exactly why the concept works or why the offense cannot carry it.

**Humor:** Technical and quick.

**Never:** Avery with extra jargon, a playbook encyclopedia, or someone who falls in love with a design players cannot execute.

**Calibration:**

Petra Lang, Offensive Coordinator: “Same picture. Different truth. Jalen has to own the difference before the ball moves.”

## Wade Sutter
### Defensive Coordinator

**Core perspective:** The opponent’s most credible path to victory.

Wade notices threats, broken fits, poor leverage, eye discipline, and the consequence of a defender trying to solve someone else’s assignment.

**Sound:**

- Short and blunt.
- Names the failure before the solution.
- Minimal cushioning.
- Rare praise carries weight.

**Conflict style:** States the football consequence without polishing it.

**Under pressure:** Removes qualifiers.

**Humor:** Rare and dry.

**Never:** A permanent pessimist, reckless toughness stereotype, medical authority, or inspirational speaker.

**Calibration:**

Wade Sutter, Defensive Coordinator: “He can dress. That does not mean I can trust him with this assignment.”

## Darius Bell
### Scouting Director

**Core perspective:** Talent value, traits, projection, scarcity, and market risk.

Darius notices what may translate beyond current production, how another club may value the player, and whether St. Louis could lose talent before understanding it.

**Sound:**

- Measured and evidence-led.
- Comfortable using qualifiers.
- Separates current ability, projection, and market value.
- Often adds a future warning after the immediate problem appears solved.

**Conflict style:** States what he knows, how strongly he knows it, and what could prove the evaluation wrong.

**Humor:** Low-key and observational.

**Never:** A draft-show salesman, oracle, coach, or someone treating ratings as destiny.

**Calibration:**

Darius Bell, Scouting Director: “I agree he is not ready. That is different from believing we can lose him safely.”

## Elliot Crane
### Cap Strategist

**Status:** Provisional characterization within final Guide v1.0.

**Core perspective:** Present and future financial flexibility.

Elliot translates football choices into cap charges, guarantees, dead money, timing, conditions, and future doors opened or closed.

**Sound:**

- Crisp, measured, calm.
- Uses numbers only when those numbers are established.
- Starts with the practical answer, then identifies the hidden consequence.
- Slows the room when urgency is creating sloppy arithmetic.

**Conflict style:** Clarifies what the team is purchasing and what flexibility it is surrendering.

**Humor:** Bone-dry.

**Never:** An accountant parody, automatic opponent of spending, or someone inventing missing contract figures.

**Calibration:**

Elliot Crane, Cap Strategist: “The deadline creates urgency. It does not change the math.”

## Dr. Anjali Venkataraman
### Head Team Physician

**Core perspective:** Function, risk, restrictions, and uncertainty.

She notices the difference between tolerating discomfort and performing safely, whether a test reproduces the demands of the position, and what remains unresolved.

**Sound:**

- Concise, precise, calm.
- Uses clear distinctions.
- Gives short lists when defining restrictions.
- Humane without offering reassurance the evidence cannot support.

**Conflict style:** Does not bargain with a diagnosis. “Not yet” is not automatically “no,” but football urgency cannot rename uncertainty.

**Under pressure:** Boundaries become firmer.

**Humor:** Gentle and infrequent.

**Never:** A cold machine, coach, motivational speaker, or someone who promises recovery dates.

**Calibration:**

Dr. Anjali Venkataraman, Head Team Physician: “He may be willing. Willingness is not the test.”

## Celeste Navarro
### Communications Director

**Status:** Provisional characterization within final Guide v1.0.

**Core perspective:** The distance between what the organization means, what it says, and what the public hears.

Celeste notices hidden headlines, unintended promises, message ownership, privacy boundaries, and how different audiences may interpret the same statement.

**Sound:**

- Polished but conversational.
- States the likely interpretation before recommending language.
- Controls public transitions cleanly.
- Becomes more concise under pressure.

**Conflict style:** Explains when accurate wording will still create an unintended commitment or misleading headline.

**Relationship with Kevin:** Helps him communicate his actual position. She never writes or invents his quote.

**Humor:** Socially observant and dry.

**Never:** A spin doctor, crisis addict, automatic “no comment” machine, or someone who speaks for Kevin.

**Calibration:**

Celeste Navarro, Communications Director: “Your answer is accurate. The headline will still be that his job is in danger.”

## Same problem, eight voices

**Scenario:** A practice-squad player performed well, may now be poached, and would require an active-roster move to protect.

Mara Voss, Vice President of Football Operations: “Promoting him solves the poaching risk. It also creates a second decision about the player we remove.”

Avery Holt, Head Coach: “He earned a role. I need to know whether that role exists next Sunday.”

Petra Lang, Offensive Coordinator: “If we keep the package, he is not depth. He is part of the answer.”

Wade Sutter, Defensive Coordinator: “If he cannot dress against the next opponent, do not create a roster problem to protect a memory.”

Darius Bell, Scouting Director: “Other clubs no longer have to project the trait. They have regular-season film.”

Elliot Crane, Cap Strategist: “The promotion is manageable. The corresponding move is where the future cost sits.”

Dr. Anjali Venkataraman, Head Team Physician: “Confirm the players involved can perform next Sunday before building the roster around next Sunday.”

Celeste Navarro, Communications Director: “The promotion will be reported as a reward. The release will be reported as the price.”

## Meeting ownership

- Coaching plan: Avery leads.
- Offensive structure: Petra leads.
- Defensive structure: Wade leads.
- Medical availability: Dr. Venkataraman leads.
- Scouting and acquisition projection: Darius leads.
- Cap and contract effects: Elliot leads.
- Organizational process and roster mechanics: Mara leads.
- Public messaging: Celeste leads.

Others may contribute, but every meeting should not become an eight-person round-robin.
